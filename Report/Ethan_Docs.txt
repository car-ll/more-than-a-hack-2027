1  THE APPROACH
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We built a heuristic possession detector that fuses three signals from
the provided data, with no machine-learning model and no training data
from any game.  The design was chosen for two reasons: (1) the ball is
missing on 41 % of graded frames, so any single-signal approach breaks
immediately, and (2) the ranking metric (spellF1) punishes fragmented
possession sequences far more than it rewards per-frame accuracy, which
means smoothing and structural cleanup matter more than a fancier frame-
level classifier.

The pipeline has four stages:

  Stage 1 — Multi-player control scoring (from tracking)
    For every frame where the ball is visible, we compute an aggregate
    "control score" for each team.  Every player within a 15-metre
    control radius of the ball contributes an inverse-distance weight
    (1 / (1 + d)), and the single nearest player receives an additional
    3× bonus.  The team with the higher aggregate score gets the frame.
    
    This is a deliberate improvement over the reference baseline, which
    uses only the single nearest player.  The multi-player score is more
    stable in crowded areas — aerial duels, tackles and contested loose
    balls — where the nearest player can flicker between teams frame to
    frame.

  Stage 2 — Smart Tagging integration (dead ball + team hints)
    We parse the Smart Tagging XML for two orthogonal signals:
    
      (a) Dead-ball mask:  Every SET PIECES event marks a window as dead
          ball (3 seconds before the restart to 1 second after).  Kick-
          offs get a longer 8-second lead-in, because the ball is out of
          play for longer before the restart.
    
      (b) Team hints:  Possession-indicative phase codes — BUILD UP,
          PROGRESSION, BALL IN FINAL THIRD, BALL IN THE BOX, CREATING
          CHANCES, LONG BALL, ATTACKING TRANSITION — carry a Team tag
          (Barcelona → HOME, Atletic Club → AWAY).  On frames where the
          ball is not visible and Stage 1 produced no answer, we adopt
          the hint.

  Stage 3 — Temporal smoothing
    A centred majority vote with a half-width of 75 frames (3 seconds).
    This is wider than the baseline's best setting (smooth50 = 2 s) and
    was chosen to push spellF1 higher at a small macroF1 cost: spellF1
    rewards fewer, cleaner possessions, while macroF1 is nearly flat
    across smoothing windows.
    
    After smoothing, remaining NOBODY gaps within each half are forward-
    filled with the last known team label, so that ball-invisible
    stretches do not create artificial dead-ball runs.

  Stage 4 — Micro-segment removal
    Any H or A run shorter than 50 frames (2 seconds) is replaced with
    the surrounding label.  Very short segments are almost always tracker
    noise — a flickered identity or a mislocated ball — and removing them
    directly reduces the possession count, which is the dominant driver
    of spellF1.


Why this rather than something else
────────────────────────────────────
We considered and set aside two alternative directions:

  • Kloppy-based approach (test_first_pass.py).  An early prototype
    loaded the tracking through kloppy, computed nearest-player distance
    with numpy/pandas, and used a rolling median of the distance
    differential.  It produced only H, A, X labels — no dead-ball
    detection at all.  Scoring confirmed that missing D labels badly
    damages macroF1.  Dropped in favour of the current pipeline.

  • Video-based approach.  We briefly explored extracting visual cues
    from the tactical MP4 (jersey colour blobs near the ball), but the
    tracking already carries team membership per slot, so the video would
    only help where the tracking is wrong — and the tracking is automatic
    anyway.  The engineering cost was not justified.


2  ASSETS USED
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Used:
    • BAR-ATH_tactical_atd.xml    ATD metadata — team assignment of
                                  each of the 46 track slots (24 home,
                                  22 away).
    • BAR-ATH_tactical_atd.txt    ATD raw tracking — 199,319 frames of
                                  player + ball positions, normalised
                                  0–1.  Coordinates scaled to metres
                                  using pitch dimensions 105.3 × 68.0.
    • BAR-ATH_tactical_smart_tagging.xml
                                  638 automatic tactical annotations.
                                  Used for dead-ball detection (59 SET
                                  PIECES events) and team-possession
                                  hints (~288 possession-phase events
                                  with valid Team tags).

  Not used / tried and dropped:
    • Tactical video (.mp4) — not decoded.  All signals come from the
      structured tracking and tagging files.
    • Broadcast source — the broadcast ATD, video and homography were
      not used.  The broadcast is not frame-aligned to the tactical
      video, the homography is valid on only 65.8 % of frames, and
      the additional complexity of temporal registration was not
      justified by the available time.
    • Homography — not used.  Since we work entirely in normalised
      pitch coordinates (from the tracking) and do not project onto
      video frames, the camera-to-pitch matrix was unnecessary.


3  TRAINING DATA
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

None.

The system is entirely rule-based.  It uses no labelled dataset of any
kind — no hand-labelled possession data from this game or any other, no
pre-trained model, no external event data.  The 5-minute public ground
truth was used exclusively to verify that the output format was correct
and to sanity-check the scorer; it was never used for tuning parameters.

The two tuneable constants (smoothing window = 75 frames, minimum
segment length = 50 frames) were set by reasoning about the problem
structure: spellF1 penalises fragmentation, so we chose values
aggressive enough to suppress tracker flicker without being so large
that real possession changes are erased.


4  POSSESSION RULES IMPLEMENTED
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

4.1  Who has the ball

  The team with the higher aggregate control score owns the frame.  The
  score sums inverse-distance contributions from every player within
  15 m of the ball and adds a 3× bonus for the nearest player.  When
  scores are exactly tied, the nearest player's team wins.

  This means a team with more players near the ball can "own" the frame
  even when the other team has the single closest player — which is a
  crude proxy for the challenge's notion of "controlled possession"
  requiring more than a single touch.

4.2  Loose balls and contested situations

  When the ball is visible but no players are close enough to register
  a meaningful score (all players > 15 m), the frame is left as NOBODY
  and later forward-filled.  In effect, possession stays with whoever
  had it last — matching the challenge rule that "in phases where the
  ball is contested or loose, possession stays with the last team that
  had it."

4.3  Ball not visible

  On the 41 % of graded frames where the tracker lost the ball:
    1. If a Smart Tagging phase is active with a team hint, use it.
    2. Otherwise, leave as NOBODY and let forward-fill propagate the
       last known label.

4.4  Dead ball / stoppages

  Dead ball is detected from the SET PIECES code in Smart Tagging.
  Each set piece marks a window around the restart as D.  Frames
  without ball visibility that are not covered by a Smart Tagging hint
  and not forward-filled default to D.

  We do not attempt to detect dead ball from the tracking alone (e.g.
  ball speed = 0 or ball off-pitch).  The baseline approach of calling
  every ball-invisible frame "dead" conflates tracker failure with
  actual stoppages, which is why the baseline's D class is weak.

4.5  Restarts

  At kickoffs, we mark an 8-second pre-restart window as dead.  For
  all other set pieces (throw-ins, corners, free kicks, goal kicks),
  we mark 3 seconds before and 1 second after the Smart Tagging start
  time.  Possession after the restart is determined by the control
  score, not by the set-piece team tag, because the taker's team does
  not always retain the ball.


5  LIMITATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  • Ball visibility gap.  41 % of graded frames have no ball position.
    On those frames we rely on Smart Tagging hints or forward-fill,
    both of which propagate stale labels across real possession changes.
    Any transition that happens during a ball gap is missed entirely.
    With more time, we would interpolate ball position across short gaps
    (< 1 second) using player trajectories as anchors.

  • Dead-ball detection is coarse.  We only detect stoppages that
    coincide with SET PIECES events (59 of them).  Fouls, injuries,
    VAR reviews and other stoppages that are not tagged as set pieces
    are invisible to us.  This likely under-counts D labels and splits
    some true stoppages into false possessions.

  • No possession-structure modelling.  The system treats each frame
    independently (after smoothing).  It has no model of what a
    possession looks like — no notion of minimum duration, no transition
    probability, no HMM or CRF.  Micro-segment removal is a blunt
    post-hoc fix.  A proper sequence model would likely improve spellF1
    substantially.

  • The smoothing window is a global constant.  75 frames is a
    compromise: too short and possessions fragment, too long and fast
    transitions are erased.  An adaptive window (wider during open play,
    narrower near set pieces) would be better.

  • No use of player velocity or formation.  The tracking includes
    player speed, and formation shape (compact vs stretched) is a strong
    cue for possession vs counter-attack.  We use only position.

  • Smart Tagging N/A entries.  36 Smart Tagging events (including all
    throw-ins) have Team = "N/A".  We discard these entirely.  For
    throw-ins specifically, the throwing team could often be inferred
    from which player is nearest the touchline.

  • Broadcast video unused.  The broadcast angle could resolve some
    tracker ambiguities (e.g. ball visibility during aerial play), but
    the temporal alignment problem was not tackled.


6  REPRODUCTION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Requirements:
  Python 3.8+, numpy.  No other dependencies.
  pip install -r requirements.txt

Data:
  Download the match assets and unpack into data/ as described in
  DATA.md, or set MTAH_DATA_DIR to their location.

Run:
  python solution.py

  Flags:
    --window N        smoothing half-width in frames (default: 75)
    --min-segment N   minimum H/A segment length in frames (default: 50)
    --out DIR         output directory (default: evaluation/results/)
    --name NAME       output filename without .csv (default: improved_possession)

  Writes:  evaluation/results/improved_possession.csv

Score:
  python evaluation/run_evaluation.py

  Scores every .csv in evaluation/results/ against the public 5-minute
  ground truth and prints a ranked board with macroF1 and spellF1.

  The pipeline runs in about 2 minutes on a laptop — it streams 199,319
  lines of raw tracking, computes per-frame control scores, then applies
  smoothing and cleanup.  No GPU required.
